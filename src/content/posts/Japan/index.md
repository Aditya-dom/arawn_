I had never been outside India before this. My passport had nothing in it.

What follows is 18 days in Japan with [Deepak san](https://www.linkedin.com/in/deepak-a-406483226/), [Mihir san](https://www.linkedin.com/in/0xmihirsahu/), [Pundir san](https://www.linkedin.com/in/adipundir/), [Vivek san](https://www.linkedin.com/in/vwakesahu/) and [Chiranjeev](https://www.linkedin.com/in/chiranjeev-mishra/). A trip I had no part in planning, did not expect to be on and was fairly certain I would not be allowed to take.

Every one of those names is a link. They are each very good at what they do, in domains that have very little to do with each other. If you are building something, go and talk to them.

## 16 enclosures

### Clap, clap

I was working in my room when the door went.

> **Mihir:** *clap.. clap..* chal bhai, Japan chalna padega
>
> **Me:** Hain? Kya? Kyun?

He was not listening. He had not come to discuss it.

> **Mihir:** Kitne saal ka ITR hai tere paas?
>
> **Me:** Ek bhi nahi
>
> **Mihir:** Fill kar. Visa apply karna padega.
>
> **Me:** Mera to lagega bhi nahi. First country, aur wo bhi Japan.
>
> **Mihir:** Apply to kar.

That was the planning phase.[^1]

So I filed an income tax return. Not out of any sense of civic duty, not because anyone from the department had written to me, but because a man clapped twice outside my door and said the word Japan. I want to be clear about the order of events here: the ITR exists because of the trip. The trip does not exist because of the ITR.

### VFS global, bengaluru

I was not serious about any of this. I want that on record because it makes what happened later funnier and me worse. I treated the entire thing as something that was happening to Mihir and that I was filling forms adjacent to.

I stayed not-serious right up until I was sitting in the VFS Global office in Bengaluru, handing a file across a counter.

The file had 16 enclosures. Numbered.

1 year of ITR because 1 year was all I had. 6 months of bank statements, attested. Rent receipts for the room I sleep in, which is a document that exists to prove that somebody is charging me money every month for a room and therefore that I am a person. A cover letter explaining who I am, what I do, where I will sleep on each of 18 nights and when I intend to come back.

4 of us filed in Bengaluru, cross-checking each other's files like we were reviewing a PR. Chiranjeev filed from Kolkata. Pundir san filed from Delhi. 6 people, 3 cities, 1 itinerary that had to match across all of them.

Chiranjeev earns a note of his own here. For the first stretch of the planning he was the loudest voice in the group chat about not coming. He had reasons. He listed them. He listed them again. Then he quietly filed from Kolkata and boarded at Bengaluru with the rest of us.

Then I flew home from Bengaluru and the group chat went to work.

Everyone's passport had come back with a sticker in it. Mine had not. Mine had been rejected. They were very sorry. They would send photos of Japan.

I believed this for long enough to feel genuinely, physically bad about it. That is how I found out I wanted to go to Japan. I had spent 6 weeks telling everybody, including myself, that I did not care about this trip. It took the group chat and 1 fake rejection to get the truth out of me.

The visa was fine. The sticker was there.

The ITR never went away though.

Mihir san's instruction had been 2 years, minimum. I turned up with 1 because 1 was all that existed. That number has been the joke ever since.

The bit has never changed. They will ask about your ITR first. You have 1 year. You are getting rejected. You are staying behind.

They were still saying it in Japan. They are still saying it now, from their own homes, to a man who went to Japan for 18 days and came back.

Here is the one thing I actually took from the process and it has nothing to do with Japan. The application is not difficult, it is *thorough*. It wants your whole bio-data in uncomfortable detail and none of what it asks for proves you will come back. All of it proves you have been legible to your own government for a sustained period of time. Tax filed, salary traceable, address documented, employer verifiable. If you want to leave India easily, start being compliant a few years before you want to leave.

Nobody tells you this at 22. They tell you to get a passport.

## Egg tart

We started this trip with our bags 80% empty. Pockets full of yen.

The first international airport I ever stood in was Hong Kong and I never got out of it. I went through it twice and the 2 visits have nothing in common except the glass.

### 50 minutes

On the way out the layover was 50 minutes. In at 09:50, out at 10:40.

I want to be honest about how little that mattered at the time. I was 22 and I had been out of India for 4 hours.

Touching down in another country for the first time is a strange thing even when you are only passing through it. The floor is not different. The air is not different. The announcements name cities you have only ever typed into a search bar. Everybody around you is on their way somewhere you have never been. You are standing inside a country you are not allowed to walk into.

I walked the length of a terminal I had no business being excited by. I was excited by it anyway.

Then the excitement turned into an adventure, because those 50 minutes also cost Mihir san his Apple Watch.

He left it on the plane. We only worked that out once we were inside the terminal, which sounds like the good version of this story and is not. The aircraft was already being turned around. We were through into transfers with no way back to the seat. We tried the airline. We tried the transfer desk. We tried lost property. We tried every person in a uniform who would stop walking.

Find My worked perfectly. It showed us the watch. It showed us the aircraft it was sitting on. It showed us that the aircraft was parked. None of that got anybody back through the gate.

50 minutes is not enough time to do any of that properly. We did all of it anyway, at a run, in an airport none of us had ever been inside before.

We got on the Tokyo flight without it.

### 7 hours 40

Coming home was the opposite problem. The layover was 7 hours and 40 minutes. In at 13:15, out at 20:55. That is a full working day with a boarding pass attached to it.

Here is what was supposed to happen. An Indian passport does not get you into Hong Kong on its own. You need a Pre-Arrival Registration, which is free, done online and valid for 6 months. You fill a form, you get a slip, you walk out of the terminal. I had plans for those hours. Hong Kong is the cheapest place on earth to buy an iPhone and I was going to leave that airport with an 18 Pro Max.

My PAR was rejected.

So I spent all 7 hours 40 airside, which is how I came to see Hong Kong entirely through glass.

There is a joke in the sequencing that I did not notice until I was writing this down. I spent 6 weeks certain Japan would say no. Japan said yes. Hong Kong, which I had not worried about for a single minute, said no.

The iPhone is still on the list.

## Konbini

### 7:30 pm, asakusa

[Cathay](https://www.cathaypacific.com/cx/en_IN.html), both legs, on time. I am not exaggerating when I say it was a very good airline. We landed at Narita in the afternoon and were standing on the roof of our own building in Asakusa by 7:30 PM.

Somewhere between immigration and that roof we started calling each other *-san*. Deepak san. Mihir san. Vivek san. Chiranjeev san. Nobody proposed it and nobody dropped it for the next 18 days.[^2]

Deepak san booked the stay and I need to say clearly that he cooked. From the rooftop we could see the Tokyo Skytree, unobstructed, lit up, close enough that it stopped being a landmark and started being the thing outside our window. Turn around on the same roof and [Sensō-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sens%C5%8D-ji) was right there. We had walked past the shrine on the way in without fully registering it, then looked down at it from above 20 minutes later while still holding our bags.

That was the first hour. It sets an unreasonable bar and the rest of the trip knows it.

Then we went for ramen. Ikuta-an, 浅草 生田庵, which the internet and 5 tired men had jointly certified as the most famous ramen shop in Japan, a claim I cannot verify and have no interest in verifying. It was the first real meal of the trip, eaten some number of hours after the last airline meal, which is the ideal condition under which to enjoy ramen and the worst possible condition under which to be trusted about it.

After that we just walked. The Sumida river is minutes from the building so we went and looked at it, then kept going because everything was close and nothing was as close as it looked.

Which brings me to the only practical advice in this entire post: you will walk in Japan. Not "there is good walking in Japan". You *will* walk, involuntarily, far more than the map implies because the stations are enormous and the distance between the platform and the street is sometimes longer than the distance between the stations. Bring your best shoes. Not your nicest ones. Your best ones.

My phone has been counting steps for about a year and a half. The long-run average is 4,056 a day, 2.84 km. That is the number you get from a man who works sitting down.

My highest single day in Japan was 44,241 steps. 31 kilometres.

September 2026 is now the highest-step month in my phone's entire recorded history: 226,293 steps, 158 km. Japan accounts for 9 days of it.

There is a vending machine wherever you stop walking.[^3] Not a bank of them at a station. 1 machine, outside somebody's house, on a lane with nobody else on it, lit and stocked and working.

### Harajuku

Day 2 we went to Harajuku to eat a fried sandwich.

I want to defend this. Age.3xQ does a fried sandwich filled with dessert and we ordered 4 between 5 of us: crème brûlée, honey rare cheese, cinnamon apple pie and 1 plain whipped cream.

The plain whipped cream was Deepak san's.

This is the same Deepak san who, the previous evening, had booked us a building with Sensō-ji on one side of the roof and the Tokyo Skytree on the other. A man demonstrably capable of taste, standing in Harajuku in front of a menu offering him crème brûlée inside a fried sandwich, considered his options and asked for cream.

They were excellent. All 4, including his. I have no notes and no shame.

Then we went for coffee, which was the correct medical response.

On the way out to the JDM tour I bought 3 Fujifilm QuickSnaps. Disposable film cameras, Fujicolor 400 loaded in, no settings to get wrong. You point it and you wind it on. 2 for me. 1 for Deepak san.

I am not in a hurry about it. There is a version of this where I get them back 2 years from now and that is a better story than getting them back on Tuesday.

## R34

Day 2 was not over. Day 2 was, in fact, about to become the best day of the trip and arguably the best day of my life and it did this in a motorway car park.

We had booked a JDM tour. Klook, which is the least romantic sentence in this blog post and I am leaving it in because it is useful.

The message that arrives on the day is a different thing entirely.

> Hi I'm Ryosei
>
> Nice to meet you
>
> Location: Rico Land Shinonome
>
> Meeting time: 7:00 PM
>
> By joining today, you are participating in a private car club activity.

So that is the actual arrangement. You pay on a travel app. What you have really bought is 1 evening of membership in a private car club, meeting at 7 in a car park in Shinonome.

The man driving was a freelance software engineer. He had bought the R34 himself, on his own earnings and he does not keep it in a garage as a trophy. He works it. The car is an asset. It earns.

I build things whose whole job is to make money. Not one of them is a 1999 Skyline.

He was also just a nice guy. Nothing to prove.

Nearly every Japanese person we dealt with over those 18 days was the same.

Ask one of them for directions and you will see what I mean. Nobody points. They walk you there. Not to the corner. To the actual station, the actual line, sometimes the actual platform. Then they turn round and go back to wherever they were headed before you interrupted them.

That happened to us again and again. By about day 4 we had stopped being surprised by it. It should have kept surprising us.

I have met plenty of good people in India too. Most of that has been professional, in a setting where somebody had a reason to help me. This was strangers on a street with nothing in it for them.

The tour starts from the parking lot, stops at Autobacs ASM Yokohama, then actually begins.

I went into ASM intending to buy nothing. I came out with a [North Face shoulder bag](https://zozo.jp/shop/undefeated/goods/109760764/) that I had decided on within roughly 4 seconds of seeing it, a 1:64 R35 and a Ferrari Daytona SP3.

Deepak san bought the R34. The same R34. The one we were sitting in. The model has doors that open.

Then: [Daikoku PA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daikoku_Parking_Area) first. Then Tokyo Tower. Then out over the Rainbow Bridge and Shibuya crossing to finish.

If you have seen *Tokyo Drift*, you already know what Daikoku looks like and you will still not be ready for the fact that it is just there, off a ramp, on a Tuesday, full of people who have brought out something extraordinary for no reason other than that other people will be there.

### Excellent and silent

The same evening I went shopping and bought a Seiko, the [5 Sports GMT in green](https://seikowatches.co.in/products/seiko-5-sports-gmt-green-dial-hdb001k1), reference HDB001K1, caliber 4R34. I spent an unreasonable amount of time first trying to find a better deal on it, then concluded at the counter that this was fine and that I would come back to Japan properly for a watch hunt.

I am aware of what that sentence is. That is a man planning his second trip while still on his first one.

Deepak san bought a different one at the same counter on the same evening, the [5 Sports FTC](https://www.seikowatches.com/us-en/products/5sports/ftc), a limited run made with an American skate brand for its fortieth anniversary and sold only in Japan and the US. Which, after the whipped cream, I am prepared to call a full recovery.

Mihir san had lost a watch in Hong Kong the previous day. 2 of us bought watches in Tokyo. I am putting those 2 facts next to each other and leaving them there.

Clothes after that, Uniqlo and Zara and this is where something finally assembled itself in my head. The fashion sense of the average person on a Tokyo street is, without exaggeration, better than that of almost anyone I know. Not loud. Not expensive-looking. Just correct.

And it's the same thing as the cars, the same thing as the watches, the same thing as the shoes. They are excellent and they are silent about it. Nobody is telling you the Seiko is good. Nobody explains the Uniqlo cut to you. The thing is simply made properly and put on a shelf and whether or not you notice is entirely your problem.

## Sunlight prima black

I gave the 24th entirely to shopping, start to finish. That was the plan.

It started at Shibuya Scramble Crossing. We went up to the Starbucks on the second floor of the TSUTAYA building, which is where every photograph of that crossing is taken from.

The building turned out to be better than the coffee. TSUTAYA runs from the second basement to the eighth floor and most of it is given over to anime and character work: a floor of manga and figures, a lounge where people sit and play Pokemon cards, a cafe near the top that changes whichever series it is themed around.

Then Zara, where the clothes were genuinely good. Then ABC Mart for shoes.

Public service announcement and the single most useful paragraph on this blog:

**Go to GU before you go to Uniqlo.**

GU is cheaper Uniqlo. Same house, same eye, less money. Not everything carries across and I am not going to tell you the quality is identical because it isn't. But enough of it carries across that walking into Uniqlo first is a tax you are voluntarily paying.

I worked this out on the 24th. I would have liked to work it out on the 22nd.

### Not onitsuka

I had an appointment booked at [OAO HAUS Daikanyama](https://oaofootwear.com/en/pages/oao-haus), OAO's Tokyo flagship, third floor of Trelli Daikanyama, 6 minutes on foot from the station.

The appointment is not me being precious about it. They do not take walk-ins at all and slots for the following month open on the 25th.

Everybody goes to Japan and comes back with Onitsuka Tigers. I did the research and didn't, which is the most insufferable sentence in this post. I am keeping it in because the shoes are better. I bought the [Sunlight Prima in black leather](https://oaofootwear.com/en/collections/all/products/sunlight-prima-black-leather-1?variant=48024167678188) and I will buy another pair the next time I am in the country.

That is now a watch and a pair of shoes I have committed to buying on a trip I have not yet taken and I had been in Japan for 3 days.

### Where the deals actually are

ABC Mart is where the prices are. Ginza, Shibuya, Shinjuku, take your pick. If you are hunting footwear on a budget, start there and not anywhere a blog has told you to start.

My favourite, though, was Daikanyama, which is where Japanese people go for real luxury rather than where tourists go to photograph it. If you want to see what the money here actually buys, go there.

Daikanyama is one of Tokyo's wealthiest and most upscale neighbourhoods, known for quiet luxury rather than flashy wealth. I ended up outside [this Dior showroom](https://www.dior.com/en_in/fashion/news-savoir-faire/folder-news-and-events/bamboo-pavilion) while riding a Luup and somehow managed to get this picture.

I then did the overwhelming majority of my own shopping in Ginza.

I have never claimed to be consistent.

What actually came home is not in this post. Souvenirs, clothes, things for the house, things for people who did not come. I have named 4 or 5 of them across these sections and left out most of it, because there is no version of this where I list the rest and you keep reading.

## The badge

On the 25th the sixth man landed.

Pundir san, our resident gangster, had flown out of Bengaluru 3 days behind the rest of us and walked into a group that had already developed in-jokes, a shared opinion about GU and a working theory about fried sandwiches. So: for everything above this line we were 5 and from here to the end of the trip we are 6.

### Ginza place

Before any of that, Deepak san and I went back to Ginza on the morning of the 25th. New mission.

He wanted the Onitsuka Tigers with the embroidery. This is the morning immediately after the paragraph where I explained at length why I did not buy Onitsuka Tigers.

Here is the useful part. Only the Ginza store does the embroidered pair. Not Shibuya. Not Shinjuku. We found this out the way you normally find things out, which is by going to Shibuya and Shinjuku first. If that is what you want, go straight to Ginza.

We got there early to stay ahead of the queue. The queue was not bad anyway. He got the shoes.

While he was doing that I went to the Sony Store, which is in Ginza Place at the Ginza 4-chome crossing. So is Nissan Crossing. 1 building, a Sony store on some floors and a Nissan showroom on the ground, with the road cars out and an F1 car sitting there.

I did not get upstairs quickly. I stood in a car showroom photographing cars I am never going to own, in a building I had walked into to buy headphones.

Pundir san came up to the Sony store with me. I had decided on the XM6 in black before I got to Japan.

Pundir san said everybody buys black. Have this one instead.

The store manager explained what it was. The [WH-1000XM6 in Sandstone](https://pur.store.sony.jp/headphone/products/WH-1000XM6/WH-1000XM6_purchase/?s_pid=jp_/headphone/special/1000Xseries/_tsuiju_purchase_wh1000xm6), new, sold in Japan and nowhere else.

Then the catch. A Japan-only colour carries a Japan-only warranty. Buy a universal colour and you can claim it anywhere in the world. Buy this one and the claim has to happen in Japan.

We decided that was not a problem. That was a reason.

The staff were laughing with us by then. I bought the Sandstone. Pundir san was right and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

So that is now a watch, a pair of shoes and a warranty waiting for me in a country I had been inside for 4 days.

It also retires my [Sennheiser HD 450BT NC](https://in.sennheiser-hearing.com/products/hd-450btnc-black-wireless-headphones). Retired, not finished. They did years of work for me and they are going to spend the rest of their life as the pair I take when I do not care what happens to them.

### Borderless

On the 26th, which was day 2 of the hackathon, Chiranjeev and I went to teamLab Borderless. It is a digital art museum in Azabudai Hills and borderless is meant literally. There are no walls between the works. More than 75 of them run out of one room into the next so there is no route and no correct order. You walk. It keeps happening to you.

Nearly all of it is dark. The works are projected light, on the floor, up the walls, through hanging strands that you walk between. There are mirrors in most rooms so a waterfall of light reaches your feet and then carries on underneath them. A room 30 feet across reads as having no end. You are not standing in front of any of it. You are inside it, as a silhouette, along with everybody else.

That night we went to Holy Tokyo Tower. Properly this time, rather than past it at speed with a man who owns an R34. We stayed at the bottom and looked up. That was enough.

### Kamakura

On the 27th, which was the last day of the hackathon, Mihir san, Vivek san and I got on a train at Shinagawa and left the city.

Deepak san and Pundir san stayed at Toranomon Hills, where the people with hacker badges were supposed to be. Chiranjeev stayed in Tokyo. That contrast is the whole joke of the previous section, paid off by 3 men going to the beach on finals day.

Mihir san said this is the station we get off at. So we got off at that station.

Then we walked. We walked to the beach. I would like to record that we walked a great deal of it because we found out after arriving that there was a closer station.

Vivek san and I said nothing. We just looked at him.

Kamakura is unreal. I don't have a better word and I'm not going to reach for one. You can see Mount Fuji from the beach there. A mountain, from a beach, an arrangement I had not previously known was on offer.

You can. We didn't.

That was the first time Japan showed us the exact place from which the mountain is visible and then declined to produce the mountain. We did not yet know this was going to become a theme.

Give Kamakura 2 full days. We gave it a few hours and were back in Tokyo the same evening, which was a mistake we made knowingly and would make again.

## Rain gear

We left Tokyo on the Fujisan View Express, the Mt. Fuji View Express, a Fujikyu Railway sightseeing train that runs Otsuki to Kawaguchiko. The train is itself the attraction, which tells you something about how seriously this country takes the business of going somewhere.

Chiranjeev did not come with us. He went home on the 29th, 10 days before the rest of us. He missed the mountain, Matsumoto, [Kamikochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamik%C5%8Dchi), Kyoto and Osaka. That is most of the itinerary he had spent 6 weeks insisting he would not be on.

I want to lay this out in order because the order is the point. He said he was not coming. He said it often. He filed his visa anyway, from Kolkata. He boarded at Bengaluru with the rest of us. He stayed a week. He went home.

And then, from his own house in India, he started sending souvenir requests to the group chat.

Not suggestions. Requests. With specifics.

Magnets. Dolls. Showpieces. Then etcetera.

He wrote etcetera. To 5 men in another country who were by that point carrying everything they had bought for themselves.

He is the only man I know who managed to be absent from both ends of the same holiday and still finish with the longest shopping list.

We stayed near Kawaguchiko Station[^4] and went straight out to see the town. The plan was to go and look at the valley at night. A girl in a 7-Eleven told us the market shuts at 6. Every day. 6.

We went anyway. She was right.

What we did find was Pizza-La. It is a chain. It was superb. Vivek san and I ate well past the point of sense, which by this stage of the trip was less an incident than a habit.

Give Kawaguchiko 2 days. 1 is not enough and everyone who goes there for 1 night knows it by about 9 PM.

And then the actual thing. Second time of asking.

September 29. Zero visibility.

We had travelled to a lake at the foot of the most photographed mountain on earth in order to look at the mountain and the mountain was not available. Not partially. Not a ridge through cloud, not a suggestion of a summit. Nothing. We were, for all the evidence available to us, standing next to a large grey absence.

2 locations, 2 days, nobody in the group had seen it. We got on the train out of Kawaguchiko having been to Fuji twice without ever once seeing Fuji.

### The karasawa plan

Day 2 in Matsumoto was supposed to be the trek. Karasawa, up in the Northern Alps. About 12 hours on foot, which is why it needed a 5 o'clock start.

The group handed me the job of getting everyone up at 5. I took it seriously. At 5 I went round the room saying lets go. Every one of them said lets go back to me, with their eyes completely shut. Not one of them moved.

We woke up at 10.

The verdict, delivered later by the men who had not moved, was that this was my fault. You should pull us out of bed. You should make us stand up. Saying lets go is not how you wake somebody. I had done it the wrong way.

Fair enough. I should have put all of them in the bathtub.

So the trek became a walk. I am going to have to live with that sentence.

### Kamikochi

Train to Shinshimashima, then the bus up.

The weather stayed clear the whole way, which after Kawaguchiko felt like being apologised to.

The water is glacier coloured. The whole valley looks arranged rather than grown, as though somebody composed it and then left. Art was the word I used at the time and I have not found a better one since.

The bear signage is not decorative. There are bells every 500 metres and you are expected to ring them as you go. Nobody is being coy about why.

All 5 of us were still moving as one group by this point. 11 days in, that counts for something.

We did cover the valley. Kappa Bridge, Taisho Pond, the paths along the Azusa on both banks. Everything down there you can reach without starting the 12 hours.

It was good enough that I only think about Karasawa occasionally, which is to say daily.

## Tatami

### Luup

The thing we missed most in Kawaguchiko and Nagano was Luup.

Luup is a shared e-scooter and e-bike service. You unlock one at a port, ride it, leave it at a different port. 50 yen to unlock, then 15 yen a minute in Kyoto and 25 in Tokyo. We had leaned on it heavily in Tokyo, for all the distance that the JR lines and the subway get you close to but not actually to. Then we spent 4 days in the mountains with nothing to unlock.

In Kyoto we got it back and used it for everything.

### Nijo castle

Day 2 started at [Nijo Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nij%C5%8D_Castle), which is where the shoguns stayed when they came to Kyoto and where the last one handed power back to the emperor in 1867. Then Tatsumi Bridge. Then more shopping because apparently that is who we are now.

The nightingale floors do exactly what they are advertised to do. The boards are laid so they squeak, on purpose, so that nobody can get near a sleeping shogun quietly. We walked them. You cannot cross that corridor without telling the entire building where you are.

400 years old. Still works. No batteries.

[Kinkaku-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinkaku-ji) in the afternoon, the Golden Pavilion, gold leaf and a pond in front of it doing the reflection the photographs are all of.

Then Arashiyama in the evening, for [Arashiyama Tsukitouro](https://arashiyama-tsukitouro.jp/). 6 in the evening to half past 8. About a kilometre of grove lit from the ground up, bamboo cut and built into shapes along both sides of the path with the open ends glowing.

The thing worth the ticket is a 30 metre corridor with woven bamboo balls hung over it at every height, lit from the inside, the whole lot spiralling down towards your head.

1,500 bamboo balls. 1,500 yen at the gate. 1 yen a ball.

### Osusume

There is a dango shop by the bamboo forest. It was about to close. I was privately hoping it would close before we reached the counter. I had no reason for this at the time. I have one now.

We reached the counter.

Deepak san did the ordering with a confidence none of us had earned. Roasted tea for me. Matcha for Mihir san. Then Pundir san said *osusume*, which means give me whatever you recommend. Deepak san said the same for himself. 2 grown men in Kyoto asking a stranger to pick for them, in a language they do not speak, about 40 seconds before closing.

The dough is sweet potato. That is true of every one of them, whatever flavour goes on top. Mine had the roasted tea. I am still not convinced any of it was food.

I have played a fair amount of poker. That is the best hand I have ever run. We stood there and watched the exact moment it landed. Yamraj arrived on a black buffalo, running towards Pundir san.

### Fushimi inari

On the 5th we did [Fushimi Inari Taisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fushimi_Inari-taisha).

The thing nobody mentions about the gates is that the crowd is not constant. It arrives in bursts. A group comes through and the path is shoulder to shoulder, then it drains. Hold still for a minute and you get a clear run of torii with nobody in it at all.

So we stopped hunting for a gap. We started waiting out the bursts. That worked.

On the way out of Kyoto there was a white [Pagoda](https://www.mercedes-benz.com/en/innovation/milestones/mercedes-benz-classic-sl/sl-pagoda/) sitting in a pay-and-display car park.

A [Mercedes-Benz W113](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_W113), built somewhere between 1963 and 1971. The hardtop dips in the middle instead of doming and that is where the name comes from. Béla Barényi patented that roof. It was the first sports car designed around a safety cell with crumple zones and the bonnet, boot lid and door skins are aluminium.

It was parked outside, overnight, wedged between a minivan and a ticket machine.

### Shinsaibashi

Dinner was [OKO](https://www.happycow.net/reviews/oko-okonomiyaki-osaka-161067) in Shinsaibashi and I am going to write about it properly because it earned it.

1 woman runs the place. Shiho. She takes your order, cooks it on the teppan in front of you, carries it out. She keeps the floors upstairs and down moving at the same time. Alone. The whole thing runs like clockwork and I still do not understand how.

The inside is not like anywhere else I went in Japan. Quirky, loud, colourful, all of it her own work. You pour your own drinks at a station by the stairs.

The menu has vegan okonomiyaki and vegan yakisoba. The vegan okonomiyaki is built on soy pulp instead of wheat flour. It was one of the best things I ate on the whole trip. By that point I had eaten a great deal.

The man behind the counter used to work on a trading floor. Now he cooks curry in Nishinari. His website says, in those words, that his curry is the best. Having eaten it, I am not going to be the one to argue.

He talked to us for a long time, mostly about his rizz. Stories about his charm and about how reliably it has worked for him over the years. I believed all of it. The man traded for a living and now he can cook. Obviously it works.

He put us on [his Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/De1aILUh7IN/).

## The way back

October 9. Narita.

Everybody was finished. 18 days of walking had arrived all at once and the whole group went under more or less as soon as we boarded. I had a window seat. Cathay had pushed a mail the day before asking us to choose one. That is when I took the window. I was asleep too, already missing a country I had not left yet.

Then I woke up. Nothing woke me. I have no explanation for it and I am not going to invent one.

I looked out of the window and there was a mountain. For about 2 seconds it was just a mountain. That is embarrassing to write down and it is what happened. Then I noticed people in the rows ahead of mine standing up with their phones out.

Fuji. All of it. The crater open at the top, the old scar of the side vent sitting on the right flank, the cone bare and brown with no snow on it yet, cloud broken up in pieces around the lower slopes. Behind it the whole plain, towns and lakes laid out flat.

3 times this country had put me exactly where you are supposed to see that mountain from. The beach at Kamakura. The lake at Kawaguchiko. The correct side of the Shinkansen. 2 refusals and 1 tip through cloud.

Then it showed me the whole thing from a seat I picked off a reminder email, on a morning I had slept through, while 4 other men slept through it as well.

I was the only one in the group who saw it. I have the only photographs of it. I took as many as I could before it went behind the wing.

I had never left the country before this. To get out of it I had to prove to a government, on paper, that I was allowed to go. 1 year of returns, bank statements, 6 weeks of forms.

Hong Kong would not even let me out of its airport. I saw that city twice and both times through glass.

Japan said yes. Then it spent 18 days saying no to the one thing I most wanted to see, right up until the last morning.

I am going to read that as an invitation. Hokkaido, Fukuoka, Hiroshima, in whatever order, with whoever wants to come. Solo, duo, all 6 of us again. I do not mind.

I am also going to call it a flex, a fluke, a miracle. Whatever it was, it was all of them.

Never say no to a trip in your early 20s. Especially an international one. Never.
